How to find the source

  1. Find the pitch. Stand where the whine is loudest and start the mic. The whine shows as a thin vertical line in the waterfall and a sharp peak in the spectrum. Tap it to lock it. Use Play target tone to make sure it is the sound you hear (keep the volume low).
  2. Hunt. Walk slowly with the phone, holding it the same way, and bring the bottom of the phone within a few inches of each device: power supplies, UPS units, power bricks, monitors, LED drivers, transmitter and amp gear. The level climbs roughly 6 dB each time you halve the distance. Near the source it jumps sharply.
  3. Log spots. If the whine comes and goes, or you want a record, leave the phone on each shelf for a few minutes with Log spots. Results ranks them for the locked tone.
  4. Prove it. Put the phone next to the suspect, then switch things off one at a time (only gear that's safe to drop off air) and tap Mark each time. If the level falls to the background, that's the source.

Things that throw readings off

  • High pitches bounce off walls and racks, so a spot across the room can read loud. Trust readings taken close up, and the switch-off test most of all.
  • Only compare numbers taken with the same phone and the same locked tone. The dB values are relative, not a calibrated sound level meter.
  • Keep talking and footsteps to a minimum during a recording.
  • The screen stays on while the app runs. If the phone locks, iPhone stops the microphone, and the recording is marked with a gap.
  • Older ears often can't hear above about 12–15 kHz. If one person hears it and another doesn't, it is probably high.
